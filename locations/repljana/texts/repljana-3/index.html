So everything [was made] of wool.

From wool, [yes], from wool.

50 (RA) a

Ah.

Everything from wool. Bags – let’s weave bags to carry the wheat,

to carry corn to the mill, and such. [Yes,] we wove bags.

53 (RA) i vrèče

And bags [too].

54 (a) i vrèče

And bags [too].

55 (RA) i vrèče i klàšni i

Bags, and homespun, and –

56 (a) vrèče vrèče sɤ̀ga zovù čuvalè tegàj ga vrèče zovèšemo

Bags, bags. Now they call them sacks, but back them we called them bags.

57 (RA) vrèče dà

Bags, yes.

58 (a) vrèče če tɤčèmo vrèče ta

Bags. We’ll weave bags, and –

59 (RA) a vɤ̀lnata ofcète go strìžat a prolettɤ̀ li lèto kogà

And [for] wool, when do they shear the sheep? Spring? Summer?

60 (a) proletɤ̀ kat mìne gerg’òvden kat se stòpli vèče

In the spring, right after St. George’s Day, when it's already warm [enough].

61 (RA) aha

Aha.

62 (a) è tegàj se strìžu

That’s when they shear them.

63 (RA) àjde na ofcète

Let’s be off to the sheep!

64 (a) ajde àjde da se da olɤ̀kne na ofcète če gim žèga

Let’s, let’s be off to the sheep, let’s lighten their load. Because they’re hot.

65 (a) ne mògu u žègutu ofcète s vḷ̀nutu

Sheep can’t take the heat when they [are still covered] in wool.
